Pre-reflection

15 Sep 2026 · Prompt 1

Why GH 101

Honestly, the main reason I picked this class is that I used to work in a hospital. Not as a doctor or nurse, so I'll let you guess :))). The job put me close enough to see how much of what happens to patients is decided outside the building: whether they can even get to us, what an outbreak does to a ward, which problems the hospital is actually built to fix. I never had time to think about any of that on shift. This class seemed like the place to do it.

That's also what interests me about global health as a field. It's not just about treating sick people in one place. It's about improving health and equity for people in completely different settings, and it counts things like poverty, housing, and environment as part of the disease, not just background.[1] I keep coming back to one question: why is the same illness annoying in one country and a catastrophe in another?

What I'm looking forward to is pretty concrete. I want a basic map of the big global health challenges and who actually carries the burden. I want to understand what people mean by "social and ecological determinants" beyond the buzzword. And I want to follow one disease all the way through, and the TB book should be good for that.[2]

What I bring, and what I want

Two things I already know a bit about: how hospitals actually run day to day, and a little epidemic and disease modeling, meaning how an infection moves through a population and what a model can and can't tell you. For a while these just sat next to each other in my head. Hospital work is local and practical; modeling is abstract and about whole populations. Global health seems to be the field that has to hold both at once. A model of spread doesn't mean much if you don't ask which communities are exposed and why. And a hospital's daily grind doesn't mean much if you never ask what pattern sent those patients through the door.

So that's what I can bring and what I want to get. I can contribute a ground-level view of hospitals plus enough modeling background to ask annoying questions when numbers come up. What I can't get on my own is the rest of it. I've never had a structured way to learn about equity, cultural humility, or the big social and ecological determinants of health.[3] That's what I'm here for. If this class works, I should be able to look at a health problem and see more than a ward or a curve.

Contribute

(non-clinical) Hospital experience. Some epidemic / disease-modeling background when spread and numbers come up.

Benefit

The global picture I do not have: determinants, equity, the main actors, and why burden falls where it falls.

Goals

I'm keeping my goals small on purpose. One for this semester, one for after. Both connect to why I signed up: I know the hospital side and a bit of the numbers side, and I want the bigger picture that ties them together.

I will actually do the reading for this class and post all three ePortfolio entries on time (15 Sep, 15 Oct, 10 Dec). I'll know it worked if, by the last entry, I can explain what "social determinant of health" means with a real example instead of just repeating the phrase.

Goal 2 (long-term)

By May 2028

I'm not planning to take more GH courses, so this one is about keeping what I learned alive. By May 2028, when an outbreak or health story comes up in the news (hopefully it doesn't), I want to be the person who asks about the conditions behind it, like who was exposed, why them, and what else is going on, not just the biology. I'll know it stuck if I catch myself doing that without being prompted.

Roadblocks

  • I'm taking 22 credits, so this class could slide to the bottom of the pile around midterms and the ePortfolio deadlines.
  • Some of the readings are outside my usual comfort zone, and it's easy to skim what you don't get.

Strategies

  • Put 15 Oct and 10 Dec on my calendar now and start each entry a week early.
  • If a reading doesn't make sense, go to office hours that week instead of hoping it clicks later.
  • For the long-term goal: when a health story comes up in the news or another class, write down one sentence about it instead of scrolling past.
